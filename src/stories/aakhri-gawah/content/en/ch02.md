The call reached the Crime Branch at 9:52 PM, and by a quarter past ten Inspector Dev Rathore was standing in the driveway of a white house in Vipul Khand with his hands in his coat pockets, looking at a bougainvillea that had been clipped into the shape of a fist.

"Somebody in this house likes control," said Kabir Ansari, following his gaze.

"Somebody in this house pays a mali to like it for him."

It had been six months since the transfer from Dehradun. Dev had asked for Lucknow because his mother lived in Aliganj and was getting old in a way that could no longer be managed by phone. Kabir had asked for Lucknow because it was home and because, as he put it, nobody else would put up with Dev's silences. In six months Kabir had learned the city's police the way he learned everything, quickly and loudly. Dev had learned the fog. He had also learned to keep his father's old felt hat on in December, which Kabir described as *Sherlock Holmes visits Aminabad*, and his reading glasses had quietly become all-the-time glasses, which Kabir had been tactful enough not to describe at all.

The SHO of Gomti Nagar, Inspector Brijesh Tiwari, came down the steps to meet them: a heavy man in a police sweater who shook hands as if he were relieved to be handing something over.

"High-profile, sir. DCP madam said Crime Branch, so." He walked them through it as they went up the steps. "Wife came home at eight-forty, found him in the study. A rectangular room. Pale carpet. One window, curtains open, black glass with the garden's darkness behind it. A tall brass trophy shaped like a flame, mounted on a heavy base, stood at the desk's right corner. A leather chair, pushed back. A visitor's chair facing the desk. The bookshelf on the far wall, floor to ceiling.

And between the desk and the shelf, Rajiv Sethi.

The field unit had lit him with a portable lamp. Dev looked for as long as he needed to and no longer.
