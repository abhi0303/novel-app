Mohan Lal's quarter stood at the back of the Sethi yard, a whitewashed room with a tin roof and a single window, three metres from the back gate and the garden tap. They did it properly. Mohan gave his consent in writing, his signature large and careful, and then stood just outside the door with his hands folded and his eyes on the ground. Kabir went through the trunk himself. Folded dhotis, two sweaters, a woollen cap. A steel tiffin with two thousand one hundred rupees in it, in small notes, rolled tight. Underneath all of it, at the bottom of the trunk, flattened by everything above it, was a bundle of red cloth, tied with a string.

Kabir untied it.

The chain lay in the cloth like something asleep. It was heavy gold, the deep orange-yellow of twenty-two carat, made of thick links that would have hung low on a man's chest. At the end of it, the size of a large coin, was a round medallion, engraved all over in a pattern like a sun or a many-petalled flower, the lines of it worn soft at the edges by years of skin. In the cold little room it seemed to give off a warmth of its own.

Folded inside the cloth with it was a receipt.

*RATAN JEWELLERS — Aminabad, Lucknow — Since 1962*
*Repair Order No. 4471*
*Customer: Shri Rajiv Sethi*
*Item: Gents' chain, 22K, with medallion*
*Work: Clasp repair*
*Received: 27/11 — Delivered: 05/12, 7:42 PM*
*Bearer: Mohan*

Tiwari let out a long breath through his nose, a sound of deep satisfaction.

"Sir," he said, and that was all, but the word contained a gold chain in a thief's trunk, a missing envelope, a back-gate key, an evening nobody could account for, and twenty years of being right nine times out of ten.

Dev looked at Mohan.

Mohan had raised his eyes from the ground for the first time. He was not looking at the policemen. He was looking at the chain in the red cloth, and his face held an expression Dev had not expected to see there. It was not fear of being caught. Dev knew that look, and this was not it. It was something closer to the way a man looks at a snake he has been living with in the walls of his house.

Ratan Jewellers was in a narrow lane off Aminabad's main bazaar, between a shop selling wedding cards and a shop selling nothing but buttons.
