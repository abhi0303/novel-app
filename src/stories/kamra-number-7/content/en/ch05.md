The hotel's CCTV system lived in the manager's office: a cramped room on the ground floor behind reception, with an old DVR box, a small monitor, and on the wall a framed plan of Silver Pine dated 1928.

Rohan ran the system himself. He was good at it.

"Sir, the DVR is old," he said, fingers moving over the keyboard. "Last night the power went out completely twice before the generator came on. Each time, part of the recording got corrupted. Nothing survived from the inside cameras. This one clip from the main gate is the only complete one."

"The basement camera?" asked Dev.

"There's a gap there too, sir. I'll show you, but first this."

He pressed play.

@scene cctv-umbrella | CAM 01, main gate. 22:14:07.

The screen showed a greenish, grainy black-and-white image: the two gate pillars, the two lamps on top of them blurred into balls of light by the rain. On the left, the guard's cabin, with a figure sitting in its window, head bowed. And in the middle, the wet road.

Timestamp: **22:14:07**.

A man came into the frame. He walked fast, a little hunched, as if ducking the rain. He held an umbrella that, even in this lifeless black-and-white world, was clearly red. The camera's colour sensor is weak at night, Rohan explained, but it still picks up a deep red.

The umbrella was tilted low. The face was completely hidden. A tall man, broad shoulders, a dark coat. He went out through the gate, crossed the road and walked out of the frame, towards the parking lot.

Nine seconds. Then black and white specks began to dance across the screen.

"That's it?" said Kabir.

"That's it, sir. Between 9:40 and 10:31, these nine seconds are all that survived. The rest is corrupted."

"Play it again," said Dev.

Rohan played it again. And again. The third time Dev stopped it at 22:14:09, with the man right in the middle of the gateway.

"Face?"

"Not clear, sir," said Kabir, leaning in close to the monitor. "But the height and build could match Arjun. He's tall, broad in the shoulders. So is Arjun."

"Could match and does match," said Dev, "are two different things."

"Sir, I'm only..."

"Dr. Kapoor is tall too. I'm tall. Rawat's brother is tall, I've seen him." Dev didn't take his eyes off the screen. "This man has three things we can see: his height, his coat, and his umbrella. Half of Mussoorie has the first two. Let's talk about the third."

He looked at Rohan. "Is the DVR clock accurate?"

"Yes, sir. It syncs over the internet."

Dev took out his phone and compared it with the clock running on the DVR screen. "Eight seconds fast. Fine." He turned to Kabir. "Write it down. 10:14, give or take eight seconds. This time is solid."

Kabir wrote it, and then added on his own account: *The first time that's actually solid.*

"The red umbrella," said Dev. "Where is it in the hotel?"

Rohan paused for a moment. "There's a red umbrella in the stand at reception, sir. From lost and found."

The three of them went to reception. By the door stood an old brass stand holding three of the hotel's black umbrellas with the Silver Pine logo. And between them, one red umbrella: long, wooden-handled, a little old.

A young man from Payal's team was still at the hotel. He put on gloves, took the umbrella out and opened it slowly. The outside was dry. But inside, deep in the folds, the fabric was still slightly damp. Droplets of water were caught between two of the ribs.

"It was used last night," he said. "Definitely. Water inside the folds doesn't dry in a day."

Under the reception counter was a thick register: *Lost & Found*. Kabir opened it and turned to October.

*02/10 — Red umbrella, wooden handle — lobby, near sofa — entry: Rohan M.*

Kabir read it out, then looked up. "Red umbrella... and the name: Rohan."

His eyes went straight to Rohan. "The umbrella is yours?"

"No, sir," Rohan said at once, but calmly. "Not mine. On the second of October I found it lying by the sofa in the lobby. Some guest must have forgotten it. I made an entry in the register; it's the rule. Nobody came to collect it. It's been in the stand since. Sometimes a guest needs one, takes it, puts it back."

"So anyone could have taken it."

"Yes, sir. Anyone. The stand is right by the door."

"And was anyone at reception last night after ten?"

Rohan looked at Neelam, who was standing behind the counter. Neelam's face went red. "Sir, I told you. I was in the back office. From ten until about 10:20, maybe."

Dev nodded. Twenty minutes. An open door. A red umbrella in the stand. And nobody watching.

They called Bahadur in. He looked at the CCTV image, then saw his own figure slumped in the cabin. His ears turned red.

"Saab." He bowed his head. "Shall I tell you the truth? Old songs were on the radio. Mohammed Rafi. The sound of the rain, the cabin was warm... I dozed off for a little while. The first time in fifteen years, saab. I swear."

"Did you see anyone go out through the gate?"

"No, saab. When I woke up... I don't know. People come and go. The parking is right across the road."

Dev let him go. Kabir said quietly, "A guard who was asleep. A receptionist heating up her dinner. Twenty minutes when nobody in this hotel was watching anybody."

"And a DVR that broke down that very night," said Dev.

"Because of the storm, sir."

"Yes. Because of the storm."

Rawat was standing by Arjun's car: a black Fortuner, now in the hotel's parking, plastered with Kolhukhet mud. Arjun had handed over the keys without objection. "Look at whatever you want, Inspector."

On the back seat were a laptop bag, a jacket and some water bottles. And under the seat, a black umbrella: an expensive brand, dry, furled.

"Sir, his umbrella is black," said Rawat.

Kabir rested a hand on the roof of the car and started thinking out loud, the way he always did.

"If it's Arjun in the footage, why did he leave his own black umbrella in the car and take the red one from the hotel stand? And if it isn't Arjun..." He stopped and looked at Dev. "Then why is someone trying to look like him?"

"Or maybe nobody's trying to look like anyone," said Dev. "Maybe it was raining, and he grabbed whatever umbrella was in front of him."

"Then who is he?"

"That's what we have to find out."

Dev wrote the CCTV timestamp in his notebook: 22:14:07. And under it he drew a line.

@clue c2

At ten that night, when the rest of the hotel had gone to sleep, Kabir was standing in the lobby by the umbrella stand. Dev saw him there and came over.

"What are you thinking?"

"Sir, one thing. If this man went out of the gate at 10:14, and the clock stopped at 10:15..."

"Then?"

"Then it looks as if someone killed Vikram, left, and a minute later the clock fell and stopped. But..." Kabir frowned. "But it takes at least two minutes to get from Room Seven to the gate. I just walked it. Stairs, lobby, gate. So if he was at the gate at 10:14, he left the room before 10:12. And the clock stopped three minutes later."

"So?"

"So either the clock stopped for some other reason. Or this man isn't the killer. Or..." Kabir threw up his hands. "Or I'm missing something."

For the first time, a faint smile crossed Dev's face.

"The third one is always true, Kabir. We're always missing something."

He looked at the red umbrella, standing quietly among the black ones in the stand as if nothing had happened.

This small clue had now become the most important part of the case. It was the first thing that had left the hotel that night with a solid time attached to it, on camera.

And in the morning, the parking records would arrive.
