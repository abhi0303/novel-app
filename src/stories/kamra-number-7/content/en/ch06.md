At eight in the morning the records arrived from the Mussoorie municipal smart parking.

The lot across from the hotel had a barrier, a token machine and a camera that read the number plate of every vehicle going in or out and saved it with the time. Kabir held the printout as if it were a winning lottery ticket.

*UK07 — Fortuner (black) — Arjun Malhotra*
*EXIT — 14/10 — 21:47*
*ENTRY — 14/10 — 22:03*
*EXIT — 14/10 — 22:41*

"Sir," said Kabir. "Out at a quarter to ten. Back in at three minutes past ten. And out again at 10:41."

Dev took the printout, read it once, folded it and put it in his pocket.

"The doctor's car?"

Kabir pointed to another line. *Honda City (white) — ENTRY 14/10 18:58.* No exit after that.

"The doctor's car was parked there all night."

"All right. Call Arjun. In the card room."

---

The hotel's old card room was in a corner of the ground floor: a square table covered in green baize, a lamp above it that lit only the table, and the rest of the room in darkness. Once, British officers had played bridge here. Now only the dust played.

@scene interrogation | The card room. One lamp, one table, and a man about to change his story.

Arjun Malhotra sat under the lamp. Today he wore a new sweater, and the lack of sleep was plain under his eyes. Dev sat opposite him, on the dark side. Kabir stood in the corner with his notebook.

"Mr. Malhotra," said Dev. "Yesterday you told us you left the hotel at 9:45."

"Yes."

"And that you didn't meet Vikram again that night."

"Yes."

"The CCTV shows a man leaving the hotel at 10:14. He has a red umbrella."

For the first time, tension showed in Arjun's face. Very slight: a muscle in his jaw that tightened for a moment and then relaxed.

"I left at 9:45," he said. "I don't know who's on your camera."

Kabir stepped forward and laid the parking printout on the table, in the light of the lamp.

"Your car's parking record shows movement after ten. Back in at 10:03. Out again at 10:41."

Arjun looked at the paper. Once. Twice. Then he put his hands on the table and laced his fingers together.

For a few seconds the room was silent. Somewhere outside a crow called.

"I came back," Arjun said at last.

"Why?" asked Dev.

"Vikram called me. I'd only just reached the Kempty Road turn. Maybe 9:54. He said, 'Come back. It's important.' That's all. Then he hung up. That's how Vikram was. He gave orders, and after an order he never had time to hear questions."

"So you came back."

"Around 10:05." Arjun glanced at Kabir, as if he might find some help there. "I came as far as the lobby. But I didn't go to his room."

"Why not? He'd asked for you."

"Because..." Arjun stopped. "Because at dinner he'd humiliated me in front of everyone. He was selling the hotel without even asking me. And then he makes one phone call and summons me like a servant. I stood in the lobby getting angry, then less angry. Then I went and sat in the car. It was raining. I smoked. I thought I'd go up, then I thought I wouldn't. Then around 10:40 I said to hell with it, and I left."

"Thirty-six minutes," said Kabir. "In the car. In the rain."

"Yes."

"Did anyone see you?"

"I don't know."

Dev pulled his chair forward a little, so that his own face came into the lamplight too.

"Why didn't you tell us before?"

Arjun lowered his eyes.

"Because there was a business dispute between us. Everyone knows. Last week we had a row in the Delhi office in front of everyone; his secretary heard it. I knew the police would suspect me first. And I thought, I didn't meet him anyway, so what does it matter whether I was in the parking lot or in Kolhukhet?"

"What it matters, Mr. Malhotra," said Dev, "is that now we don't know which of the things you say are true."

Kabir asked, "Did you ever threaten Vikram?"

Arjun's answer came instantly. "No."

So instantly that Kabir drew a line under that "No" in his notebook.

---

Dev took a sheet of paper from his file: a photocopy of the audit printout found on Vikram's desk. He placed it under the lamp and put his finger on the Himgiri Constructions line.

"What's this?"

Arjun looked at the paper. This time the muscle didn't just tighten. It stayed tight.

"A vendor," he said. "They do renovation work. In our hotels."

"What renovation?" asked Dev. "Not one wall in Silver Pine has been painted in the last two years. It's written in your own hotel's register."

"That was... work at the Delhi hotel. And Dehradun. I don't remember the details. The accounts team handles it."

"In the margin of this paper Vikram wrote: 'Since when?'"

Arjun said nothing.

"Would you like to say anything?"

"I'd like to speak to my lawyer," Arjun said at last. His voice was completely cold now. "If this is a business question, business people will answer it."

"Of course," said Dev, and put the paper back in the file. "But remember one thing, Mr. Malhotra. Your lawyer can save you from a fraud charge. Not from a murder charge."

For the first time Arjun looked Dev straight in the eye.

"I didn't kill Vikram, Inspector."

"Nobody has said yet that you did."

---

After Arjun left, Kabir opened the card-room window. Cold air came in and the lamp swayed slightly.

"Sir," he said, and Dev knew a theory was coming. "If you ask me, Arjun is the strongest suspect."

"Go on."

Kabir began counting on his fingers.

"One. Motive. Vikram had found out that Arjun had pulled a ₹4.2 crore fraud. If the hotel was sold there'd be an audit, and everything would come out. Two. Lies. First he said he left at 9:45, then he admitted coming back at 10:05. Three. Opportunity. He was in or near the hotel from 10:05 to 10:41, and he says himself there's no witness. Four. The CCTV. At 10:14 a tall, broad-shouldered man went towards the parking lot with a red umbrella. Arjun's car was in the parking lot. Five. The clock stopped at 10:15."

"And?"

"And he says he never went to the room. But suppose he did. There's a fight with Vikram, something happens, he leaves around 10:12, goes out of the gate at 10:14, sits in the car wondering what to do now, and drives off at 10:41."

Dev nodded, like someone checking a child's homework.

"It's a good story. Now hear the holes. One: the man on the CCTV hasn't been identified. Two: if Arjun killed Vikram before 10:12, how did the room end up bolted from the inside? Only Vikram, or someone inside, could slide that bolt. Three: how was Vikram killed? No injuries, throat clean. Four: if he was running away at 10:14, why sit in the parking lot for the next twenty-seven minutes, where anyone could see him? And five: the clock."

"What about the clock, sir?"

"You worked it out yourself last night. A three-minute gap. That gap is still there."

Kabir pulled a face. "The post-mortem will tell us how he was killed."

"Yes. And until then," said Dev, "being a strong suspect and being the killer are not the same thing."

---

They left the card room. In the corridor Rohan stood with a clipboard, going over today's menu with Thapa. Neat and calm, as always.

Dev stopped.

"Kabir," he said quietly. "What did Arjun say? How far did he come at 10:05?"

"To the lobby, sir."

"Neelam was in the back office then. Bahadur was asleep in his cabin. So who was in the lobby at 10:05?"

Kabir looked at Rohan.

"Neelam said Rohan was on his rounds at ten," he said slowly. "Then he went to the basement at 10:10."

"If Arjun came into the lobby," said Dev, "and Rohan was on his rounds, Rohan would have seen him."

"And he didn't tell us."

Just then Rohan looked up, saw Dev, and gave a small bow and a namaste. Then he went back to his list.

"Call him in," said Dev. "And get the key to the basement."
