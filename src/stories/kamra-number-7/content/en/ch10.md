On the morning of the third day, Dr. Sameer Kapoor was called in again.

This time he brought a brown file with him: Vikram's medical history, fifteen years of it. Dev had asked for it the night before. The doctor laid the file on the table and rested his hand on it the way a witness rests a hand on a holy book.

"Thank you, Doctor," said Dev. "Please sit."

The doctor sat. Today he wasn't wearing his cardigan but a brown coat. The handkerchief was the same. Kabir noticed it had grown a little grubby from all the folding.

"You told us you saw Vikram drinking tea at 9:50," said Dev. "The waiter delivered the tea at 10:05. No tea had gone to Room Seven before that, all evening. The kitchen has a record. So what were you referring to?"

The doctor took off his glasses and began to clean them, very carefully, as if there were a mark on the lens that only he could see.

"I may be wrong," he said. "I met him earlier that evening."

"When?"

"After dinner. Nine, half past nine, perhaps. In his room. He was drinking water, perhaps, or something else, not tea. I... I assumed it was tea. He always drank tea."

"Why didn't you make that distinction in your statement? That you met him earlier, and it wasn't tea?"

"It was a stressful night, Inspector." The doctor put his glasses back on. "A man I'd known for fifteen years was lying on the floor in front of me. I tried to save him and couldn't. I didn't remember the exact time. I said the first time that came into my head."

"9:50 is a very exact time, Doctor. Ten minutes to ten."

The doctor said nothing.

The doctor's face changed.

Not by much. But for a moment the colour drained from his cheeks, as if someone had dimmed a light inside him.

"That was my medical kit," he said. "Vikram had certain medicines prescribed."

"What is this medicine? In plain words."

The doctor's voice changed. Now he was speaking as a doctor, and here he was at ease.

"It keeps the heartbeat under control. It gives a weak heart a little more strength. It's a very old medicine, Inspector, more than a hundred years old. At one time it was made from a flower. The doctor looked at Dev for a moment, as if working out where this question was going. Then he answered directly.

"With this medicine, the line between a dose and a poison is very thin. That's why I brought it myself. I didn't send it with anyone else."

"In the dark, in a hurry, running to save a man's life, Inspector, who checks whether the seams are inside or out?"
