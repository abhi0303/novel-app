SI Rawat had pinned his theory to the library wall.

He had sent to the hotel store for an old corkboard, propped it against a bookcase, and covered it with three days' worth of photographs, tied together with red thread and drawing pins. At the top in the middle was the door of Room Seven. Arjun on one side, Rohan on the other. Meera and the doctor below. A photograph of the red umbrella. A photograph of the clock, with a "?" scrawled on it in marker. And from Arjun to Rohan ran a thick red thread with a yellow note caught in the middle.

@scene case-board | SI Rawat's case board. Red threads tying everything together.

"Sir," said Rawat, with a hidden note of triumph. "Look at this first."

He handed Dev a thin file. On the cover: *Silver Pine Hotel — Staff Records — Rohan Malhotra.*

"I got Neelam to open the HR cupboard. This is Rohan's personnel file."

Dev opened it. Kabir looked over his shoulder.

:::doc Personnel Record — Silver Pine Hotel
Name: Rohan Malhotra
Date of birth: 03/1996
Address: Rajpur Road, Dehradun
Father's name: —
Mother's name: Late Smt. S. Malhotra
Previous employment: Ganga View Resort, Rishikesh — Asst. Manager (2019–2025)
Joined: 05/2025 — General Manager
Reference / Recommended by: Mr. Arjun Malhotra (Partner)
:::

"Recommended by," Rawat read aloud, "Arjun Malhotra."

Kabir flipped back through his notebook at once. The first day's statement. *"You and the manager are both Malhotras. Any relation?" — "No. No relation."*

"He told us there was no relation," said Kabir. "And here it says he's the one who got Rohan the job."

"And Rohan hid Arjun's 10:05 visit," Rawat added. "Because Arjun told him to. Sir, these two are on the same team."

Kabir's eye stopped on another line in the file. *Father's name: —*. A blank dash.

"The father's name is blank," he said.

"Lots of people's are, sir," said Rawat, with a wave of his hand. "They skip it when they fill in the form. The mother's name is there."

Kabir wrote *Father: blank* in his notebook and moved on.

---

Rawat began his theory. Kabir, who at first was only listening, slowly got drawn in, like a man who stands on the bank for a while and then wades into the water.

"Listen, sir," said Rawat. "Arjun knew Vikram had found out about the fraud. If the hotel was sold there'd be an audit, and Arjun would go to jail. So Arjun had to stop Vikram. He couldn't do it alone, so he roped in his man. Rohan. Who he'd got the job for himself, who owed him."

"And Rohan had the master key," said Kabir. "To every room."

"Exactly. Now look. Arjun leaves at 9:45, in front of everyone, so everyone sees. Then at 10:03 he quietly comes back. Rohan meets him in the lobby and doesn't log him in. Arjun goes upstairs and meets Vikram. There's a fight, or whatever. Vikram's heart was weak. A shove, a hand on the throat, or a pillow. The doctor says a weak heart can stop from anything."

"There were no marks on the throat," said Dev.

"A pillow doesn't leave marks, sir." Rawat pressed on. "Then at 10:14 Arjun leaves with Rohan's red umbrella. Rohan's umbrella, sir, his name is in the register. Arjun goes to his car and sits there for twenty-seven minutes working out his alibi. Meanwhile Rohan goes down to the basement, switches off the camera and writes 10:12 in the register to prove he was downstairs. The message? Maybe Arjun sent it to himself from Vikram's phone, so it would look later as if Vikram called him and he didn't go."

"And the door?" asked Dev. "Bolted from the inside?"

Rawat smiled. This was what he had been waiting for.

"A trick with the bolt, sir. Rohan must have loosened the screws in the frame beforehand. Then at 10:55 he put on a show of breaking the door in front of Bahadur. Whoever went in first could do anything. And who went in first? Rohan. He sent Bahadur off for the doctor and did whatever he needed to do on his own. Maybe that's when the page disappeared."

Kabir thought for a moment and said, "And the clock, he also... no, the clock had already stopped." He shook his head. "But fine, leave the clock out. Everything else fits, sir."

Then he said, all at once, everything that had been going round in his head for three days.

"Arjun wanted to take over the business and hide the fraud. First he said he left at 9:45, then he admitted he came back at 10:05. He has a motive. Rohan could help him, because Rohan had the master key. The red umbrella was Rohan's too. Someone could have faked the message. I think Arjun and Rohan did the murder together."

For a moment the library was silent. Rawat patted Kabir on the back.

Dev had let them both finish. He hadn't interrupted once. Then he took out a cardamom pod, bit down on it, and asked:

"What's the biggest assumption in your theory?"

Kabir thought. "That the man on the CCTV was Arjun."

"The second?"

"That Rohan helped him."

"The third?"

Kabir paused. "That the message was faked."

"And Rawat-ji's fourth," said Dev. "That there was a trick with the bolt." He picked up Payal's report from the table and held it out to Rawat. "Have you read this? The screw holes are split. The wood broke fresh, in one go. If someone had loosened the screws beforehand, the holes would be round and smooth. Bahadur, who has known that door for fifteen years, says it didn't move an inch. The door really was bolted from the inside, Rawat-ji."

Rawat read the report. The glow on his face dimmed a little.

"Sir, maybe..."

"Maybe." Dev closed the file. "You have four assumptions. Not one of them has been proved. But you've joined them together into a complete story. A good story. I liked it myself."

Kabir gave a small smile and said, "So what's your theory, sir?"

Dev didn't answer.

He just took the four suspects' statements out of the file and laid them side by side on the table. Arjun. Meera. Rohan. The doctor. Four sheets in a row.

"Up to now we've been finding out who's lying," he said. "The answer is: all four. Arjun said he didn't come back; he did. Meera said she was in her room; she wasn't. Rohan hid Arjun. The doctor hid his time." He touched each sheet once. "Now we have to find out which lie was necessary for the murder. And which lie is only covering some other secret."

---

Dev sent for Arjun. Just for one question.

"Did you get Rohan the job at Silver Pine?"

Arjun frowned. "Yes. So?"

"You told us there was no relation between you."

"There isn't, Inspector! A recommendation isn't a relation." Arjun's voice rose. "Last year I met him at a hospitality conference in Rishikesh. He was an assistant manager at some resort there. I liked him. Educated, straightforward. He came up and talked to me himself, said he wanted to work at Silver Pine. His name was Malhotra too. I thought, he's one of ours, why not. Vikram needed a new manager, so I gave him the name. What's wrong with that?"

"He came up to you himself?"

"Yes. Said he'd heard a lot about Silver Pine. The oldest hotel in Mussoorie. That's all."

Rohan was asked as well. He said without hesitation, "Yes, sir. Arjun sir helped me. I owe him. That's why, when he told me not to make an entry, I didn't refuse. Maybe that was my mistake."

Every answer fitted neatly into place. Every single one.

---

That night Kabir and Dev were alone in the library. Rawat's corkboard still leaned against the wall, the red threads gleaming in the lamplight.

"Sir," Kabir said quietly. "I accept the theory has holes. But you have to admit it all fits together a bit too neatly. Arjun and Rohan. The recommendation. Hiding the entry. The master key. The umbrella."

"I admit it."

"So?"

"So when something fits together very neatly, Kabir, there are only two possibilities. Either it's true." Dev looked at the board, at the thick red thread that ran from Arjun to Rohan. "Or someone has worked very hard to make it fit."

Kabir was about to say something when Dev's phone rang. The name on the screen: *Anita Joshi.*

It was nine at night. Anita never phoned at night.

Dev put it on speaker.

"Rathore." There was no tiredness in Anita's voice today. There was something else. "The toxicology is back. The priority screen, not the full one, but what we needed is there. Your man's heart was weak. But it didn't stop on its own."

Her voice rang clear in the silence of the library.

"Someone gave it a push. We've found poison."
