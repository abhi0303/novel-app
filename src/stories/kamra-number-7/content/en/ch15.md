Behind the counter at Norbu Woollens stood an old desktop computer with a picture of the Dalai Lama as its wallpaper. Norbu's grandson explained to Kabir over the phone from Delhi how to pull up the recording: three times, slowly, as if to a child. Kabir had understood the first time, but he kept quiet.

By the time Dev arrived, Kabir had the folder for the night of the fourteenth open.

"It's a colour camera, sir," said Kabir. "It picks up a bit of colour even at night, because there's a sodium light over the parking lot. And look at this. The angle covers the whole lot, and the stretch of road by the hotel gate as well."

He started the recording at 22:13.

The screen showed the parking lot bathed in orange light. Slanting lines of rain. The blurred colours of the prayer flags fluttering overhead. A few cars. In the corner, Arjun's black Fortuner, dark. In the middle, a white Honda City.

**22:14:21.** From across the road, from the direction of the hotel gate, a red umbrella entered the frame. The same walk, the same hunched shoulders as on the hotel camera. It crossed the road into the lot and went straight to the white Honda City.

"The doctor's car," Kabir whispered.

**22:15:20.** The man with the umbrella stopped by the car door. He pressed the key button. The headlights blinked once. He opened the passenger door, and the small light inside the car came on.

The man leaned in. The umbrella tipped backwards. And for two seconds, only two, the yellow light inside fell on his face.

Greying hair. Glasses, whose lenses caught the light and flashed for an instant.

Kabir froze the frame.

Dr. Sameer Kapoor.

In the frame he was opening the glovebox. Over the next few seconds he took out a paper bag: white, with a green cross on it. A pharmacy bag. He closed the door, the light went off, and he turned back towards the road, juggling the umbrella.

**22:18:49.** The red umbrella crossed the road towards the hotel gate and left the frame.

Kabir pushed his chair back and looked at Dev. "The face under the red umbrella, sir. It wasn't Arjun. It was the doctor."

"And he wasn't running away from the hotel," said Dev. "He went to fetch something from his car."

A printout of the frame was laid on the table in front of Dr. Kapoor: his own face, in the yellow light of the car, his glasses flashing.

This time he didn't deny it.

"Yes. It was me," he said. There was a strange relief in his voice, as if someone had lifted a heavy bag off his shoulder. "I'd left Vikram's new medicine in the car. I got there at seven, it was raining, I grabbed my bag and the pharmacy bag stayed in the glovebox. At dinner Vikram asked, 'Kapoor, my medicine?' I said I'd give it to him in the morning. He said, 'Tonight. The old one is running out.' So after ten I went. There was nobody at reception, there was a red umbrella in the stand, I took it."

"Then?"

"Then I came back. Around 10:19. At the gate Bahadur jumped up and saluted, poor man, half asleep. I went upstairs. Meera came to Number Nine at 10:20. I told her, 'Wait five minutes, I'll give Vikram his medicine and come back.' I went to Number Seven at 10:22. Gave him the bottle. Five minutes. At 10:27 I was back in Number Nine."

The room went quiet. Kabir's pen was racing.

"So you were in Room Seven at 10:22," Dev said slowly. "Alone. With Vikram. With his tea. And with a bottle of the same medicine."

"Yes."

"And Mrs. Sethi told us you were with her the whole time."

"Meera lied." The doctor closed his eyes. "For me. When she found out the poison was the same medicine I'd brought, she was afraid of... of what you'd think. What you're thinking now."

"And your 9:50?"

The doctor opened his eyes.

"I didn't want anyone to know I was in Number Seven after 10:20. Then everyone would ask where I went afterwards. And the answer was... Meera. So I moved the time back. Some time after dinner. The first one that came into my head." He let out a bitter breath. "And the tea... the tea was true, Inspector. When I went in, he was drinking tea. He had the cup in his hand. That's why 'I saw him drinking tea' came out of my mouth. The time was a lie. The tea was true."

Kabir looked at a line on the first page of his notebook, something he had heard Dev say on the first day and had written down out of habit.

*A liar doesn't always say something false. Sometimes he's only hiding something true.*

"When you went in at 10:22," Dev asked, "how was Vikram?"

For a moment the doctor's face trembled, as if something were about to break. Then he steadied himself.

"Angry. As always." He looked down. "I gave him the bottle, told him to start the new dose in the morning, and left."

Dev looked at him for a long time. The doctor didn't raise his eyes.

In the corridor Kabir caught hold of Dev's arm. Kabir never did that.

"Sir. The doctor was in Number Seven at 10:22. With the medicine. With the tea. Alone. He lied to us twice, and Meera lied for him too." His voice was full of excitement and exhaustion at once. "The theory was right, sir. Only the time was wrong. Meera wasn't keeping watch in the corridor at 10:20, she was waiting in Number Nine. But the doctor went in. He put the medicine in the pot, and..."

"And handed Vikram a sealed bottle?" asked Dev.

"Maybe he used something else from his kit..."

"Maybe."

SI Rawat had joined them. "Sir, now an arrest is justified. The doctor, at least."

Dev looked at both of them.

"Wait one day," he said. "Two things are coming. The count of the tablets in the bottle. And the report on the residue in the teapot: which tablet the medicine came from. If the doctor used his own bottle, there'll be tablets missing from it. If he used something from his kit, the residue will tell us." He gently took Kabir's hand off his arm. "One day, Kabir. Sending an innocent man to jail takes a day. Bringing him back takes years."

In the evening Kabir was back at Norbu's shop. He was collecting printouts of the doctor's frames when his eye caught another part of the recording. He skimmed forward without thinking, and then stopped. Then went back.

He called Dev.

"Sir. There's something else on this camera. Arjun's car."

"Go on."

"Arjun says he sat in his car from 10:05 to 10:40. Smoking. Sir, I've watched all of it. His Fortuner arrives at 10:03. Arjun gets out and walks towards the hotel at 10:04. After that the car is completely dark. Nobody visible inside, no red glow of a cigarette, and in the rain that glow would show up clearly, sir. Nothing. Three-quarters of an hour, an empty car."

"Then?"

"Then at 22:39 a man comes. But not from the hotel's main gate. From a narrow lane on that side of the road, which..." Kabir paused, picturing the 1928 plan of the hotel. "...which comes from the hotel's side gate at the back. By the garden. If you come straight down the service staircase, that's the gate you reach."

At the other end Dev was silent for a while.

"Is the man Arjun?"

"He's tall, black umbrella, gets straight into the Fortuner. At 22:41 the car leaves, which matches the parking record." Kabir took a breath. "Sir, Arjun wasn't sitting in his car. He was inside the hotel. For three-quarters of an hour. And he didn't leave by the main gate, he left from the back."

"Then where was he?" asked Dev. From his voice, he seemed to be asking himself rather than Kabir.

"I don't know, sir." Kabir looked at the frozen frame on the screen: a tall man, a black umbrella, coming out of a dark lane towards his car. "But Room Seven is right next to the service staircase."
