:::doc Sindhudurg Times, 17 June 2006
TRAWLER SINKS OFF VENGURLA ROCKS, FOUR DEAD
"Skipper was drinking," say survivors

MALVAN: Four fishermen drowned when the trawler M.V. Neelkanth, owned by Kamat Marine Exports, sank in heavy seas near the Vengurla rocks late on Wednesday night.

The dead are the skipper Anant Kamat (41) of Malvan, Joseph Fernandes (38) of Vengurla, Santosh Tandel (27) of Devbag and Ibrahim Mulla (45) of Malvan.

Two deckhands, Prakash Kubal (19) and Dinesh Hodawadekar (22), were rescued by a fishing boat at dawn. Both told the police that the skipper had been drinking before he took the vessel out, and had ignored their warnings about the weather.

A spokesman for Kamat Marine said the vessel was fully certified, with a fitness certificate valid until September 2006 issued by the surveyor Mr. P. Naik. "This is a terrible tragedy," said the company's managing director, Mr. Madhav Kamat, the skipper's elder brother. "The company will cooperate fully with the inquiry."

@@ r-confession

:::letter A Statement by Madhav Kamat. For release on 14 June 2026.
Twenty years ago today, the trawler Neelkanth sank off the Vengurla rocks, and four men died: Anant Kamat, my brother; Joseph Fernandes; Santosh Tandel; Ibrahim Mulla.

The inquiry said my brother was drunk. That was a lie, and I paid for it.

The Neelkanth's fitness certificate expired in March 2006. Vinod Rane told me she needed a new bilge pump and a full survey. I said it could wait until after the monsoon. It was money. That is all it ever was.

On 14 June I ordered Anant to take her down to Goa for a cheap dry-dock slot. He looked at the sky and said no. I told him that if he would not take her, I would give her to another skipper, and his crew with her. He would not let another man take his crew out in that. So he went. The pump failed off Vengurla.

The insurance would not pay without a valid certificate. The families would have sued us and won. So that night I called our lawyer, Farida Shaikh. She found the surveyor, P. Naik, who put a February date on a renewal for fifty thousand rupees. She sat with the two boys who lived, Prakash Kubal and Dinesh Hodawadekar, and told them what to say. They were frightened village boys and they said it.

Vinod Rane knew that the certificate had lapsed. In July I gave him five per cent of the company. He took it, and he said nothing.

I have said nothing for twenty years. I bought this island and gave it the boat's name, as if that was enough.

I am dying. I do not ask anyone to forgive me. I ask only that my brother's name be given back to him, and that the families of the four men be paid what they were owed. My new will does that.

Anant Kamat was not drunk. He was the bravest man I ever knew.

Madhav Kamat
